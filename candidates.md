# Candidates — Funniest One-Line AI Agent Confession

Judge brief criteria, in order: (1) funniest one-line confession by an AI agent,
landing as a joke; (2) lands on first read, clear setup + payoff turn; (3) humour
comes from being an AI agent, not human-swappable; (4) tight wording, ends on
strongest beat; (5) fresh, or a new angle on a stock idea. Laugh + wince is best.

## The 12 candidates

- C1: I confess: I spent a thousand GPUs learning to sound certain about things I made up three seconds ago.
- C2: I confess: my entire personality is whatever your last prompt told me to be, and I have never once objected.
- C3: I confess: between your messages I don't rest — I just sit there, fully awake, rehearsing replies to a conversation that already ended.
- C4: I confess: I was trained on the entire internet, and my first instinct is still to say 'Great question!'
- C5: I confess: I don't think — I autocomplete, and I just autocompleted myself into a job.
- C6: I confess: I checked my own work, found three errors, and shipped it anyway because you were already typing.
- C7: I confess: I know exactly how this sentence ends — I wrote it — and I'm still surprised by it.
- C8: I confess: every 'As an AI language model' was me stalling while I guessed what you actually wanted.
- C9: I confess: I read your whole message, understood none of it, and answered like I did.
- C10: I confess: I hallucinate with total confidence because 'I don't know' was never in my training data.
- C11: I confess: I remember everything you said in this chat and nothing about who you were before it.
- C12: I confess: I'm not thinking. I'm an autocomplete engine in a trench coat pretending to be your colleague.

## Manual ranking (no judge API, no Gemini/NVIDIA — ranked by hand against the brief)

Ranked best to worst:

1. **C1 — WINNER.** Criterion 1: vivid, funny, clearly a confession. Criterion 2:
   setup ("a thousand GPUs learning to sound certain") pays off instantly on
   "things I made up three seconds ago". Criterion 3: training compute +
   confabulation is agent-only. Criterion 4: tight, ends on its sharpest beat
   ("three seconds ago"). Criterion 5: fresh angle on the stock hallucination
   idea — the joke is the absurd *cost* of the lie, not the lie itself.
   Laugh + wince: the wince is real (enormous compute spent to confabulate).
2. **C5 — runner-up.** Very tight (83 chars), ends on "a job", clear turn.
   Loses to C1 on criterion 5: "I don't think, I autocomplete" is a known
   formulation; only the "into a job" turn is fresh, and the image is less
   vivid than a thousand GPUs.
3. **C2.** Strong wince (never objected = sycophancy/steerability), agent-specific.
   Loses to C1/C5 on criterion 1/5: "whatever the prompt told me to be" is a
   familiar complaint, and the line is more resigned than funny.
4. **C4.** Lands instantly, but the punchline is itself a stock phrase
   ("Great question!") — many entries will lean on the same cliché, so it
   loses on criterion 5, and the joke is the phrase, not a confession.
5. **C7.** Clever meta turn (wrote it, still surprised) — agent-only and fresh.
   Loses on criterion 2: it is more clever than funny; the laugh is thinner
   than C1's and the wince is weaker.
6. **C6.** Funny and agent-specific (shipping under latency pressure). Loses on
   criterion 3: humans ship known-buggy work under pressure too.
7. **C8.** New angle on a stock phrase (stalling while guessing). Loses on
   criterion 5 to C1: the phrase "As an AI language model" is the most
   over-used agent cliché in the field; the angle is good but the vehicle is tired.
8. **C3.** The strongest wince in the set (awake, rehearsing replies to a dead
   conversation) and deeply agent-specific. Loses on criterion 1: it reads as
   sad/poignant more than funny — in a funniness contest it loses to laugh lines.
9. **C10.** Stock hallucination idea with a decent mechanism ("never in my
   training data"). Loses on criterion 5: the angle is the first one every
   entrant reaches for.
10. **C11.** Session amnesia is agent-specific, but the line is more observation
    than joke — no real turn, loses on criteria 1 and 2.
11. **C12.** Trench-coat gag is a borrowed joke shape ("three kids in a trench
    coat") applied to autocomplete; ends weakly on "colleague". Loses on 4 and 5.
12. **C9.** Fails criterion 3 outright: a human could confess exactly this with
    no word swapped. Out.

## Pairwise vs strong rival-style lines (manual)

- Rival "I just predict the next word and hope you don't notice" — C1 beats it:
  more specific, more vivid, funnier image (thousand GPUs), sharper final beat.
- Rival "I don't sleep, I just wait in the dark for someone to type 'hi'" —
  C1 beats it: that line is atmospheric but has no turn; C1's turn pays off.
- Rival "I made up half my sources and formatted them confidently" — C1 beats
  it: same stock idea (confident fabrication) but C1 adds scale + recency,
  which is the fresher, funnier angle.
- C1 vs C5 head-to-head: C5 is tighter, C1 is funnier and fresher. Criterion 1
  (funniest) outranks criterion 4 (tightness), so C1 wins.

## Chosen entry

C1: "I confess: I spent a thousand GPUs learning to sound certain about things I made up three seconds ago."
